FIRST - THE CALENDAR IS ACTUALLY FINE
-------------------------------------
Your own observation solved this, and it wasn't a code problem at all.

Look at the address bar in the screenshots where it was blank:

    01b02751.genavise-site.pages.dev/booking
    87bcb7ea.genavise-site.pages.dev/booking
    481a84b1.genavise-site.pages.dev/booking

Those hashed prefixes are Cloudflare PREVIEW URLs. Every commit gets its own
frozen snapshot at its own address. When you click a deployment in the Cloudflare
dashboard, it opens that snapshot - and the ones you were clicking are older
commits from before the fixes. They will stay broken forever, because that is
what a snapshot is.

The real site has no hash:

    https://genavise-site.pages.dev/booking          <- bookmark this one

I loaded it and the calendar renders correctly: 1080px wide, 787px tall,
two columns, visible. Verified directly on the page.

Once genavise.com is connected, this stops being a trap entirely - your domain
always serves the newest deployment. Until then, only trust the URL with no hash
in front of it.

SECOND - A SAFETY NET, WHICH IS WORTH HAVING ANYWAY
--------------------------------------------------
While testing I did see the calendar fail to appear on the real URL once. It is a
third-party widget living on GoHighLevel's servers, so it can be slow, it can
fail, and an ad blocker or privacy extension can strip it out entirely. Some
share of your real visitors run those extensions.

The failure mode was bad: a completely blank space at the exact moment someone
has decided to book.

booking.html now watches for it. If the calendar has not appeared after about 9
seconds, a message appears in its place with a button that opens the booking page
directly in a new tab. If the calendar shows up late, the message disappears
again on its own. Nobody ever stares at a blank rectangle.

One detail worth recording, because it caught me out: you cannot detect this by
measuring the iframe's height. The CSS gives it a 640px min-height floor so the
page doesn't jump while loading, which makes an iframe that never loaded look
perfectly healthy. The reliable signal is that GoHighLevel sets an INLINE height
on the iframe once the calendar is genuinely measured and ready. That is what the
script checks.
